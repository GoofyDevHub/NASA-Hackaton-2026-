# NASA judging and submission — working checklist

This document helps the team review its proposed entry. It records targets and pending checks. Scores and prior project reviews are internal estimates.

The [judging guide supplied by the team](references/nasa-judging-awards-guide.pdf) lists eligibility on pages 3–4 and the five 1-to-5 criteria on pages 5–7. The live judging and challenge pages returned access errors during this repository setup. Use the supplied PDF and the most recent successful brief review, then recheck any changed official instructions before submission.

## Judging evidence to prepare

| Criterion | Current foundation | Evidence still to deliver |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | Accessibility-centered exploration, optional descriptions and sight-free playlist design | Observe independent navigation and whether listeners recognize an image change. Record participants, tasks, results and limitations truthfully |
| Creativity | Deterministic piano mapping, spatial traversal, a visual Earth interface and tactile controller concept | Show the contribution of each element and the reason for the musical rules. Describe established methods and original design choices accurately |
| Validity | Reproducible reference scores, WAVs, source images and editable concept geometry | Test the integrated app, matched A/B processing, offline startup, accessible controls, simulator faults and loop continuity |
| Relevance | NASA images drive the music, with EIC-linked sources in the selected catalog | Lead with an EIC-linked example. Explain the source legend, what is mapped and what change is audible |
| Presentation | Shared visual direction and a proposed 29-second cut | Final English project page, clear story, finished demonstration, captions, transcript, references and readable controller exploded view |

Every technical or user-benefit claim should point to evidence. Describe the controller as a concept and simulator until the corresponding physical functions have been built and measured. For the warm/cool cue, explain whether its meaning follows calibrated data or a contextual convention.

## Eligibility and required submission checks

- [ ] Both teammates are registered, listed on the team's Members tab and assigned to the same Local Event or Universal Event. Check waiting-list status and any applicable affiliation review.
- [ ] Select the official challenge, The Earth Information Jukebox.
- [ ] Complete every required submission field.
- [ ] Provide the required demo, using the proposed 29-second film and checking its actual duration against the guide's 30-second limit. The alternative allowed by the supplied guide is a presentation of up to seven slides, including title and introductory slides.
- [ ] Use English on the project page and in linked materials. A video spoken in another language requires English subtitles.
- [ ] Check the applicable deadline and local judging schedule. The supplied guide states November 15, 2026, 11:59 p.m. in the Local Event's timezone, or the Team Owner's timezone for the Universal Event.
- [ ] Review and accept the required submission, terms and privacy declarations.
- [ ] Review the original-work and resource-permission declaration. Resolve the reuse conditions for existing materials using the official instructions or organizers' clarification.
- [ ] List external images, code, text, tools and audio resources in the References field, including freely available resources.
- [ ] Record specific AI contributions and check the disclosure requirements for each submitted asset.
- [ ] Open linked materials without login and check that they match the submitted version.
- [ ] Submit using the project page and record the actual result.

The supplied guide permits teams of 1–6 for a Local Event and 2–6 for a Universal Event. The current two-person team meets the headcount condition; the registration checks remain pending.
